THE ORIGINAL SHELF — SEPTEMBER 19, 2026

Four nostalgic programs from the big build day: what a 1990s DOS toy looks like today. Each is a single Python file with its own self-test. The files came over on Nov 5, 2026 and every self-test passes — three of the four are below; the bouncing line is still on Steven's machine.

Acidwarp Tribute

warp.py · 15 self-test checks · numpy + pygame
IN THE COLLECTION · SELF-TEST PASSED

Draw one picture of palette-index numbers from maths — rings, spirals, waves, plasma, kaleidoscope, tunnel, a Julia fractal — then rotate the colour palette every frame so the colours flow through the shapes. Fade, new picture, repeat.

Download warp.py

Bouncing Line

bounce.py · 17 self-test checks · numpy + pygame
STILL ON STEVEN'S MACHINE

A line bouncing off all four walls with a fading trail. After a random wait, the next bounce is special: one line splits into two to four, and several lines kill the one that bounced. A remake of Steven's seventh-grade first program. It didn't come over in the Nov 5 handover — its card is saved for it.

Mandelbrot Zoomer

fractal.py · 12 self-test checks · numpy + pygame
IN THE COLLECTION · SELF-TEST PASSED

A Mandelbrot/Julia zoomer with colour cycling. Starts as a screensaver dive into the spiral valleys; click to explore. A full 320x200 frame takes about 12 milliseconds — it needed a maths coprocessor in the 1980s.

Download fractal.py

Morse Code Tool

morse.py · 19 self-test checks · also MorseCode.exe (23 MB)
IN THE COLLECTION · SELF-TEST PASSED

Type text, hear Morse code. Speed, tone, repeat count, Farnsworth spacing, hide-and-reveal for learning by ear, and save as WAV. Made for Steven's father, a ham radio operator since 1956; the exe was built for the ham club.

Spectrum Search

spectrum-search.html · added Nov 8, 2026 · runs right in the browser
GOES LIVE WITH THIS PAGE

Search that learns how your mind takes in information, then changes how results are shown — with your permission, asked in plain language.
